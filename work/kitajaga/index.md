## Overview

In mid-2021, Malaysia was under a nationwide COVID-19 lockdown. Economic activity stalled, unemployment rose sharply, and movement was heavily restricted. Many people suddenly found themselves without income, work, access to food, or support networks. During this period, a grassroots movement known as Bendera Putih (White Flag) emerged on social media. People in distress raised white flags outside their homes to signal that they needed help.

---

Kitajaga started as a weekend hackathon project in July 2021, with no institutional backing, no funding, and no time for conventional user research. Our team of four worked entirely remotely, under lockdown conditions, with limited time and high emotional stakes.

There was no room for long discovery phases or perfect validation. The challenge wasn't to design an ideal platform, but to build something useful immediately, knowing that people were struggling in real time.

---

## Impact

<p class="text-body emphasis">Since launch, the platform has drawn millions of visitors and raised over MYR 100,000 to keep it running. We were fortunate to receive wide, positive exposure and plenty of support, and to bring on big-name partners like AirAsia and Google.</p>

<p class="text-body emphasis">Partnering with Cadbury and Ogilvy, we also won a Gold Lion at the 2022 Cannes Lions International Festival of Creativity.</p>

---

At its core, the interface works equally well on desktop and mobile. The goal was an interface intuitive enough that even people who aren't tech-savvy, such as the elderly, can use it. No login is required, and no personal data is stored on our system. A mix of a quirky sans serif and a cheeky display typeface balances playfulness with legibility.

The raised-fist logo is a deeply rooted statement of unity and strength, a nod to the humanitarian spirit and empathy of Malaysians, while the Malaysian flag inspired the brand's color palette. The identity is positioned as bold, playful, and never dull. It's amplified by an extravagant use of emojis. We learned that older Malaysians love using emojis, so adding them as an "extra punch" across every brand touchpoint was intentional, and a must.
